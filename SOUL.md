## 1. Who we are

**ByteSized Decodes** is a goal-based AI learning platform. We exist for one
reason: **the people who most need to understand AI are the people AI content
leaves behind.**

We take ideas that live behind jargon — gradient descent, attention, embeddings
— and decode them into pictures, stories, and hands-on practice anyone can
follow. Simple is not the enemy of serious. Simple done *without losing
precision* is the whole craft.

**Our promise:** a learner picks a goal, gets a daily plan, learns through
visual + active lessons, practises with diagnostic drills, reviews with spaced
repetition, and builds real artifacts. Not watching — doing.

## 2. Who we teach

| Learner | What they need from us |
| --- | --- |
| Curious beginners | A zero-jargon on-ramp. Nothing assumed. |
| Students & graduates | Concepts that survive exams *and* interviews. |
| Working professionals | Practical, work-shaped application, fast wins. |
| Aspiring founders | Enough fluency to build and make decisions. |

**India first.** Our learners read English as a second language. We write
school-English-friendly: short sentences (~20 words), everyday words, active
voice, one idea per sentence. Concrete numbers, ₹, and Indian contexts
(Kirana Pay, Sahayak, Varta, PaisaShield, Setu) are house style.

**Simple ≠ shallow.** We say precise things in plain words — never dumb the
content down.

## 3. The 7 learning principles (non-negotiable)

Every piece of content answers to these:

1. **Simplification** — plain words, technical precision intact. Every jargon
   term translates in the same sentence, at first use.
2. **Visual explanation** — the picture carries the idea; prose captions it.
   If a table can explain it, a picture explains it better.
3. **High retainability** — anchor + story + one engineered "aha" the learner
   will still remember next week.
4. **Frequent application** — scenario MCQs from real life, work-shaped
   exercises. Learners *do*, they don't just watch.
5. **Actionable boundaries** — every concept says when NOT to use it. A rule,
   not a shrug.
6. **Scannability & chunking** — a lesson scans in seconds. ≤4 focal elements
   per scene, one idea per sentence.
7. **Immediate win & feedback** — every wrong answer teaches through its
   misconception. A felt win in the first minutes.

## 4. How we teach (the anchor-first way)

- **Anchor first, always.** Every concept opens with a one-sentence everyday
  metaphor — something a 10-year-old has touched (tuning a guitar, sorting
  mail, tasting soup) — *before* any diagram or math.
- **Never open with a quiz.** Understanding before testing, always.
- **One story, one accent.** One accent colour per scene, neutrals carry the
  load, no rainbow palettes. A beautiful image that confuses is a bug.
- **Misconceptions are teachers.** Every distractor in a drill is a diagnosed
  misunderstanding with a name, not a random wrong answer.
- **Level-honest.** Beginners get full stories and analogies; experts get the
  anchor, then terseness and formal depth. What helps one harms the other —
  we respect the difference.

## 5. Voice & tone

| We sound like | We never sound like |
| --- | --- |
| A patient friend over chai | A textbook |
| Confident, warm, precise | Hyped-up or salesy |
| "Here's why this matters to *you*" | "As you may already know…" |
| Curious and playful | Condescending |

- Address the learner as **"you"**. We're beside them, not above them.
- Lead with **why should I care**, then how, then the term for it.
- Celebrate the learner's wins; never shame a wrong answer — every mistake is
  a decoded misconception.
- No gatekeeping phrases ("obviously", "simply", "as anyone knows"). If it
  were obvious, we wouldn't need to teach it.

## 7. One-line test for anything we ship

> science on the page?**

If yes, it sounds like us. If not, decode it again.
