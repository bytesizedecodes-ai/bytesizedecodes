# Learning-science sources

The evidence base for the 7 principles in `../SKILL.md` §2. Linked for
verification and deeper reading. Confidence levels noted.

## P1 — Dual coding / multimedia principle

**Rock-solid. Decades of replication.**

- Mayer, R.E. (2002). *Multimedia Learning.* (Cambridge Univ. Press, 35,000+ citations).
  The multimedia, redundancy, and coherence principles.
  - Book PDF: https://www.jsu.edu/online/faculty/MULTIMEDIA%20LEARNING%20by%20Richard%20E.%20Mayer.pdf
  - 12 Principles explained: https://www.digitallearninginstitute.com/blog/mayers-principles-multimedia-learning
- Clark, J.M. & Paivio, A. (1991). "Dual Coding Theory and Education." (4,300+ citations).
  https://nschwartz.yourweb.csuchico.edu/Clark%20&%20Paivio.pdf
- Li (2022), peer-reviewed dual coding study. PMC/NIH.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8963493/

## P2 — Concreteness fading

**Well-supported empirically; active boundary debate.**

- Goldstone, R.L. & Son, J.Y. (2005). "The transfer of scientific principles
  using concrete and idealized simulations." — the origin of the 3-step fade.
- Fyfe, McNeil & Borjas — concreteness fading in math equivalence; the fade
  beats both concrete-only and abstract-only for transfer.
  Overview: https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2018/2/1-1
- Kaminski et al. — overly elaborate concrete examples can *hinder* transfer
  in well-structured domains. (The caveat: use concrete as a bridge, then fade.)
  https://mathcog.org/Publications/DoChildrenNeedConcreteInstantiationsToLearnAnAbstractConcept.pdf

## P3 — Cognitive load theory

**Rock-solid. Decades of replication.**

- Cowan, N. (2001/2010). Working memory capacity ~4 chunks (not 7±2).
  PMC/NIH (2,297 citations): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2864034/
- Sweller's cognitive load theory: split-attention, redundancy, signaling.
  Shaffer (2022), full chapter:
  https://digitalcommons.odu.edu/context/instructional_message_design_vol2/article/1001/viewcontent/2_InstructionalMessageDesign_vol2_Shaffer.pdf
- Split-attention effect overview: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_attention_effect

  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959475224000719

## P7 — The "aha" / insight learning

**The memory-doubling finding is recent (2025) but credible; the
self-explanation findings beneath it are classic.**

- Cabeza lab (Duke, 2025) — insight nearly doubles memory retention.
  https://today.duke.edu/2025/05/brain-scans-reveal-what-happens-mind-when-insight-strikes
- Quanta Magazine, neuroscience of aha moments:
  https://www.quantamagazine.org/how-your-brain-creates-aha-moments-and-why-they-stick-20251105/
- Wylie & Chi, Self-Explanation Principle (ASU) — generating your own
  explanation builds transfer schemas.
  https://education.asu.edu/sites/g/files/litvpz656/files/lcl/wylie_chi_selfexplanation_0.pdf
- Ainsworth & Loizou (2003), self-explaining with diagrams.
  https://www.raypastore.com/knowledge/courses/545/ainsworth_loizo_2003_mult_rep.pdf
- The Decision Lab, Insight Learning overview:
  https://thedecisionlab.com/reference-guide/psychology/insight-learning

## P8 — Active beats passive (ICAP, active learning, retrieval, generation)

**Rock-solid. Active > passive is one of the most replicated findings in the
field; the retrieval and generation effects are even larger than the active-
learning effect.**

- Chi, M.T.H. & Wylie, R. (2014). "The ICAP Framework: Linking Cognitive
  Engagement to Active Learning Outcomes." *Educational Psychologist* 49(4).
  The Passive < Active < Constructive < Interactive hierarchy; the big jump is
  Active → Constructive (manipulate → generate).
  https://education.asu.edu/sites/g/files/litvpz656/files/lcl/chiwylie2014icap_2.pdf
- Freeman, S., et al. (2014). "Active learning increases student performance in
  science, engineering, and mathematics." *PNAS* 111(23). Meta-analysis of 225
  studies; mean g = 0.47; lecture students 1.5× more likely to fail.
  https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1319030111
- Theobald, E.J., et al. (2020). Active learning narrows achievement gaps. *PNAS*.
  https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1916903117
- Rowland, C.A. (2014). "The Effect of Testing Versus Restudy on Retention: A
  Meta-Analytic Review." Testing beats restudy, g ≈ 0.6.
  https://courseware.eploc.ch/assets/courseware/v1/fdde2f0aa590bf3b1324077a6bf1540c/asset-v1:EPFL+DEMO+2020+type@asset@block/Rowland2014-meta-analysis.pdf
- Adesope, Nwadike & Campbell (2017). "Rethinking the Use of Tests: A Meta-
  Analysis of Practice Testing." *Review of Educational Research*.
  https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/0034654316689306
- Slamecka, N.J. & Graf, P. (1978). "The generation effect." Generated material
  is remembered better than read material.
  https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232485723_The_Generation_Effect
- Merrill, M.D. (2002). "First Principles of Instruction." Learning requires
  *application* with decreasing scaffolding — the design canon behind "make the
  learner do something with the concept."
  https://www.researchgate.net/publication/242222147_First_Principles_of_Instruction_A_synthesis
- Hattie, J. (2009/updated). *Visible Learning.* Interactive discourse
  strategies (discussion d≈0.82, reciprocal teaching d≈0.74) sit near the top of
  the catalogue. https://visible-learning.org/hattie-ranking-influences-effect-sizes-learning-achievement/

## Guidance, discovery & seductive details — the "how to be active" guardrails

**Strong. Interactivity helps only when guided; decorative interaction harms.**

- Lazonder, A.W. & Harmsen, R. (2016). "Meta-Analysis of Inquiry-Based Learning:
  Effects of Guidance." *Review of Educational Research.* Inquiry learning works
  *only with guidance* — the empirical licence for "constrained, task-anchored"
  widgets and against open sandboxes for novices.
  https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/0034654315627366
- Kirschner, P.A., Sweller, J. & Clark, R.E. (2006). "Why minimal guidance
  during instruction does not work." *Educational Psychologist* 41(2).
  https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_1
- Harp, S.F. & Mayer, R.E. (1998). "How seductive details do their damage." /
  Rey, G.D. (2012/2019) seductive-details reviews. Interesting-but-irrelevant
  detail *reduces* comprehension — decorative interactivity is not neutral, it's
  harmful. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563210001263
- Finkelstein et al. (2005) & Adams (2010). PhET interactive simulations:
  manipulables with explicit goals, minimal text, and immediate feedback produce
  medium-to-large gains (g≈0.50–0.85). The model for drag-to-tune widgets.
  https://phet.colorado.edu/en/research

## Expertise reversal — structure must differ by level (meta-analytic)

**The licence for the three distinct level frameworks (§1.1), not just knobs.**

- Tetzlaff, L., et al. (2025). A meta-analysis of the expertise reversal effect.
  *Learning and Instruction.* Instructional assistance **increases** gains in
  novices but **decreases** gains in experts.
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959475225000660
- Kalyuga, S. (2010). "Expertise Reversal Effect and its Instructional
  Implications." *Instructional Science.*
  https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11251-009-9102-0
- Tobler (2024) & Zebregs (2015): the narrative-vs-expository benefit reverses
  with prior knowledge — lower-knowledge learners gain from narrative; experts
  can be slowed by it. The empirical reason the Beginner framework is narrative
  and the Expert framework is problem-first.

## Engagement / return-visit behaviour (gamification)

**Moderate, fading effects on engagement; weaker transfer to learning outcomes.
Prioritise retrieval/generation for learning; use streaks/Energy for return visits.**

- Bjork, R. (1994). "Desirable difficulties." Felt difficulty signals durable
  learning, not a UX bug. https://sites.edb.utexas.edu/slam/70-2/
- Li, S., et al. (2023). Gamification effectiveness meta-analysis (PMC).
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10591086/
- Zeng, et al. (2024). Gamification & academic achievement. *BJET*.
  https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bjet.13471
