# Learning-science sources

The evidence base for the 7 principles in `../SKILL.md` §2. Linked for
verification and deeper reading. Confidence levels noted.

## P1 — Dual coding / multimedia principle

**Rock-solid. Decades of replication.**

- Mayer, R.E. (2002). *Multimedia Learning.* (Cambridge Univ. Press, 35,000+ citations).
  The multimedia, redundancy, and coherence principles.
  - Book PDF: https://www.jsu.edu/online/faculty/MULTIMEDIA%20LEARNING%20by%20Richard%20E.%20Mayer.pdf
  - 12 Principles explained: https://www.digitallearninginstitute.com/blog/mayers-principles-multimedia-learning
- Clark, J.M. & Paivio, A. (1991). "Dual Coding Theory and Education." (4,300+ citations).
  https://nschwartz.yourweb.csuchico.edu/Clark%20&%20Paivio.pdf
- Li (2022), peer-reviewed dual coding study. PMC/NIH.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8963493/

## P2 — Concreteness fading

**Well-supported empirically; active boundary debate.**

- Goldstone, R.L. & Son, J.Y. (2005). "The transfer of scientific principles
  using concrete and idealized simulations." — the origin of the 3-step fade.
- Fyfe, McNeil & Borjas — concreteness fading in math equivalence; the fade
  beats both concrete-only and abstract-only for transfer.
  Overview: https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2018/2/1-1
- Kaminski et al. — overly elaborate concrete examples can *hinder* transfer
  in well-structured domains. (The caveat: use concrete as a bridge, then fade.)
  https://mathcog.org/Publications/DoChildrenNeedConcreteInstantiationsToLearnAnAbstractConcept.pdf

## P3 — Cognitive load theory

**Rock-solid. Decades of replication.**

- Cowan, N. (2001/2010). Working memory capacity ~4 chunks (not 7±2).
  PMC/NIH (2,297 citations): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2864034/
- Sweller's cognitive load theory: split-attention, redundancy, signaling.
  Shaffer (2022), full chapter:
  https://digitalcommons.odu.edu/context/instructional_message_design_vol2/article/1001/viewcontent/2_InstructionalMessageDesign_vol2_Shaffer.pdf
- Split-attention effect overview: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_attention_effect

## P4 — Expertise reversal effect

**Rock-solid. The foundation of the audience-level model.**

- Kalyuga, S. (2007). "Expertise Reversal Effect and Its Implications for
  Learner-Adapted Instruction." (1,500+ citations).
  https://www.uky.edu/~gmswan3/EDC608/Kalyuga2007_Article_ExpertiseReversalEffectAndItsI.pdf
- Kalyuga (2007/2010), Educational Psychology Review (Springer).
  https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11251-009-9102-0
- Rey & Buchner (2011), empirical reversal. J. Exp. Psych: Applied.
  https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2011-20756-001
- Cambridge Handbook chapter:
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-handbook-of-expertise-and-expert-performance/cognitive-load-and-expertise-reversal/03F656FD334F23214426ACB4118FEBF9

## P5 — Narrative arc in educational visuals

**Strong on practice/retention; the specific visual-lesson claim is newer.**

- Tobler (2024). "Telling Stories as Preparation for Learning." *Learning and
  Instruction.* — lower-prior-knowledge students benefit most.
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959475224000719
- Xiao (2025), narrative-based teaching review. PMC/NIH.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12213908/
- Storytelling with Data (tension/pacing):
  https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/when-tension-is-high-get-to-the-point

## P6 — Segmenting / progressive disclosure

**Rock-solid (segmenting); the "progressive disclosure" framing is UX-origin.**

- Mayer (2014), Segmenting Principle. APA PsycNet (900+ citations).
  https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2015-00153-013
- Nielsen Norman Group, Progressive Disclosure (the UX framing):
  https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/

## P7 — The "aha" / insight learning

**The memory-doubling finding is recent (2025) but credible; the
self-explanation findings beneath it are classic.**

- Cabeza lab (Duke, 2025) — insight nearly doubles memory retention.
  https://today.duke.edu/2025/05/brain-scans-reveal-what-happens-mind-when-insight-strikes
- Quanta Magazine, neuroscience of aha moments:
  https://www.quantamagazine.org/how-your-brain-creates-aha-moments-and-why-they-stick-20251105/
- Wylie & Chi, Self-Explanation Principle (ASU) — generating your own
  explanation builds transfer schemas.
  https://education.asu.edu/sites/g/files/litvpz656/files/lcl/wylie_chi_selfexplanation_0.pdf
- Ainsworth & Loizou (2003), self-explaining with diagrams.
  https://www.raypastore.com/knowledge/courses/545/ainsworth_loizo_2003_mult_rep.pdf
- The Decision Lab, Insight Learning overview:
  https://thedecisionlab.com/reference-guide/psychology/insight-learning
